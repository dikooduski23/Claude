**Why this story:** it's real and written in Cook's own journal (13 April 1769), and few people know it (the famous version is the potato-king legend, which is mostly myth). The share trigger is that parents use the same trick on kids.

```
Captain Cook got his crew eating stinky pickled cabbage... by feeding it to his officers.
Seventeen sixty-eight. Scurvy was rotting sailors' gums... and killing thousands.
Cook packed nearly eight thousand pounds of sauerkraut... and the sailors wouldn't touch it.
So Cook tried a trick.
Every day, his officers got a plate. The sailors? Only if they wanted.
Within a week... Cook had to put the whole ship on a ration.
You see, give sailors anything new... they grumble.
Let the bosses value it... and it's "the finest stuff in the world." Cook's words.
His next voyage: three years at sea... no one on his ship died of scurvy.
He didn't sell the cabbage... he sold the jealousy.
It still works on kids. All because...
```

## Fact check (every claim has 2+ sources; a separate reviewer checked it from scratch)
| Claim | Sources |
|---|---|
| The men wouldn't eat the "Sour Kroutt" until Cook had it served every day at the cabin table for all officers, leaving it optional for the men. Within a week he had to put everyone on board on an allowance (ration). | Cook's journal, 13 Apr 1769 (NLA MS1, transcribed by paulturnbull.org); State Library NSW transcript; Hektoen International (2021) |
| Quote: "the moment they see their Superiors set a value upon it, it becomes the finest stuff in the World" | Cook's journal (NLA MS1 / paulturnbull.org); State Library NSW transcript |
| Endeavour carried 7,860 lb of sauerkraut ("nearly eight thousand pounds") | Hektoen International (2021), citing Lamb; Modern Farmer (2014), citing the victualling minutes |
| Scurvy rotted gums and killed sailors in great numbers | Hektoen International; National Geographic interview with Jonathan Lamb |
| On his next voyage (Resolution, 1772-75, three years) no one on Cook's ship died of scurvy | State Library NSW ("Method for preserving health"); National Museum of Australia (Cook's letter to Pringle); Cook was given the Copley Medal in 1776 for this work |

**Left out, and why:**
- **"Made his crew beg" / "the crew demanded it":** Cook only wrote that he had to ration it within a week. "Beg" overstates that.
- **"Nobody touched it":** the officers ate it; only the sailors refused.
- **"Not one man died of scurvy" for the whole expedition:** the cook of the sister ship *Adventure*, Murdoch Mahony, died of scurvy in July 1773. The script says "on his ship".
- **The first voyage having zero scurvy deaths:** three deaths may have been scurvy (Hektoen).
- **Sauerkraut as "the cure":** its vitamin C is modest, and Cook also credited fresh greens and wort. The punchline is about jealousy, not medicine.
- **"Two million sailors died of scurvy":** widely repeated, but the evidence is weak.
- **Flogging men who refused greens:** only weak sources.
