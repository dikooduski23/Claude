A gardener stood his daughter on a giant water lily leaf, and the leaf ended up designing the Crystal Palace. The true story of Joseph Paxton and the Great Exhibition of 1851.

In 1849 Paxton's Victoria amazonica lily at Chatsworth grew leaves strong enough to hold a child, thanks to thick ribs braced by cross-ribs underneath. When the Building Committee rejected all 245 designs for the Great Exhibition hall, Paxton sketched an iron-and-glass frame based on the leaf on blotting paper during a Midland Railway meeting. Soldiers marched on the galleries to prove they were safe, and about six million people visited.
