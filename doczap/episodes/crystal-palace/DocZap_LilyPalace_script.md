# Doc Zap: "A gardener stood his daughter on a leaf… and built a palace"

**Story:** Joseph Paxton, the Duke of Devonshire's gardener at Chatsworth, England, 1849. He grew the world's biggest water lily and stood his little daughter on one of its leaves. Underneath, he found the leaf's secret: ribs and cross-ribs, like girders. A year later, London rejected every one of 245 designs for its Great Exhibition hall. The gardener sketched his idea on blotting paper, and it became the Crystal Palace.

**Why this one:** almost nobody knows the Crystal Palace started with a child on a leaf. Frame 1 is a strange, beautiful image (a little girl standing on a giant floating leaf), and the reveal is a clear cutaway: the leaf's rib pattern turns into the iron roof.

**The strange act (line 1):** a gardener stands his daughter on a floating leaf.
**Cutaway:** the leaf flips over and becomes see-through. Its radiating ribs and cross-ribs glow, then morph into the palace's iron-and-glass roof frame. Handwritten labels: "ribs" and "cross-ribs".
**Length:** about 112 words, roughly 38–41 s with Patrick.

```
A gardener stood his little daughter on a floating leaf... and it beat two hundred and forty-five experts.

Eighteen forty-nine. The biggest water lily on Earth... and it didn't sink.

You see, underneath, thick ribs ran out from the middle... locked together by cross-ribs. Like girders.

A year later, London needed a giant hall for the Great Exhibition.

Experts sent two hundred and forty-five designs. Every single one was rejected.

So Joseph Paxton, the gardener, doodled his idea... on blotting paper, in a railway meeting.

A palace... made of glass.

Critics said it would collapse. So soldiers marched on it.

It held.

The Crystal Palace. Six million people came to see it.

Nearly three hundred thousand panes of glass... on a skeleton copied from a leaf.

All because...
```

| People feared it would collapse; soldiers marched on it to test it | ✅ | Encyclopedia.com; Britain Express; Illustrated London News engraving (Mary Evans) |
| Six million people visited the Great Exhibition in the Crystal Palace (6,039,722) | ✅ | Wikipedia "Great Exhibition"; London Museum; V&A; The Gazette |
| About 300,000 panes of glass | ✅ | V&A (293,655 panes); Victorian Web (300,000); The Gazette (293,000) |

**Left out on purpose:**
- **Annie's age:** sources say 5 or 8.
- **Exact sketch date:** 11 June in most sources, 18 June in one.
- **Build time:** 22 weeks vs 39 weeks in different sources.
- **Paxton's "Nature was the engineer" quote:** only one source.
- **How she stood:** some accounts say she stood on a small tray or plank laid on the leaf, so the animation shows a small tray under her feet, which fits both versions.
