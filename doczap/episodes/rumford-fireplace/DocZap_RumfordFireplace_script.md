# Doc Zap: The Smaller Fireplace (Count Rumford, 1796)

**Format:** 30-45 s, the Rick's Clips formula: a strange act on purpose → doubted → the trick pays off → "You see," → ironic punchline that loops into line 1.
**Why this story:** a perfect contradiction (a smaller fireplace makes a warmer room); a mechanism you can see in a cutaway (heat rays bouncing off angled walls); and it's fireplace season. Few people know it.
**Look:** warm firelit Georgian rooms, red damask, brick and candlelight. This is the warm, shadowed palette that measured closest to your reference reels.

```
Count Rumford made the fireplace smaller... and the room got warmer.
Seventeen ninety-six. London's fireplaces were huge... and the rooms were still cold and smoky.
So Rumford squeezed them. Shallower. Narrower. Sides angled out.
A smaller fireplace... for a warmer room? Sounds backwards.
You see, the heat you feel from a fire is radiant heat... like sunlight.
The hot air just flies up the chimney... with the smoke.
So his angled walls bounced that heat into the room... and a narrow throat shot the smoke straight up.
In just two months... over a hundred and fifty London fireplaces were rebuilt his way.
Even Jane Austen put one in a novel. And builders still copy it today.
The fix for a cold room... was a smaller fireplace.
All because...
```

## Fact check (2+ sources each; a separate reviewer checked every line, including Rumford's own essay)
| Claim | Sources |
|---|---|
| In 1796 Rumford made fireplaces smaller and shallower, with widely angled sides (covings at 135°) and a streamlined, narrow throat (about 4 inches) | Rumford, *Of Chimney Fire-places* (1796), Ch. I-II (rumford.com transcription); Wikipedia "Rumford fireplace"; Buckley Rumford Co.; This Old House |
| Old fireplaces were huge, and rooms were cold and smoky | Rumford's essay (fireplaces that smoke; heat lost up the chimney); Jane Austen, *Northanger Abbey* ("the ample width ... of former times"); New England Today |
| The useful heat is radiant; the heated air goes up the chimney with the smoke | Rumford's essay (he estimated the heat lost in the smoke at "three or four times" the radiant heat); Buckley Rumford Co.; This Old House |
| Over 150 London fireplaces were rebuilt his way in two months | Rumford's own essay, 1796: "above a hundred and fifty fire-places in this city" altered "under my direction, within these last two months" (the primary source) |
| Jane Austen put one in a novel | *Northanger Abbey* (1817): the fireplace "was contracted to a Rumford"; Hugh Rowlinson, JASNA *Persuasions On-Line* 23.1 (2002) |
| Builders still copy it today | This Old House (popular again in restorations and new construction); Buckley Rumford Co. (still builds them) |

**Left out, and why:**
- **"250 fireplaces in two months":** the figure on JASNA and Mr Porter has no source and contradicts Rumford's own "above a hundred and fifty".
- **"Freezing" rooms:** hype; the script says "cold".
- **"Only the rich":** his fixes went into ordinary London houses too.
- **The smoke shelf:** often credited to Rumford, but it's disputed and isn't in his essay.
- **His backstory** (born Benjamin Thompson in Massachusetts, a count of the Holy Roman Empire): true, but there's no room for it.

**For the visuals:** Rumford fireplaces are TALL and shallow, not tiny squat boxes. The "smaller" is the depth and the narrow back, so the cutaway must show that, or commenters will call it wrong.
