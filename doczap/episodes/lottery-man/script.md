# Doc Zap: "He Won the Lottery 14 Times… Legally" 🎟️🤑

**Why this one:**
- **It's the Pudding Guy formula from your reels:** a normal man beats a giant system with simple maths, and it's legal.
- **It has more tension:** a race against time and a twist ending (time runs out, then the winning ticket is in the pile anyway).
- **It ends on a payoff people love to share:** the FBI and CIA investigate, find nothing, and every lottery has to change its rules.

**On-screen text hook (frame 1):** HE WON THE LOTTERY 14 TIMES… / LEGALLY

**First frame:** a Zap Freeze. Stefan Mandel stands on a mountain of lottery tickets in a warehouse while 12 printers spit out more and tickets rain down. Glowing lottery balls hang in the air. The camera orbits slowly.

**Length:** about 40 s (117 words up to "because"). Voice: ElevenLabs Patrick (Eleven v3).

## Script (paste into ElevenLabs)

[intense] This man won the lottery fourteen times... and it was completely legal.

[curious] You see, he noticed something nobody else did. Sometimes the jackpot was worth more than buying every possible ticket.

[excited] In nineteen ninety-two, Virginia's jackpot hit twenty-seven million dollars. Buying every combination cost about seven million.

[determined] So his team printed millions of tickets... and raced from store to store.

[panicked] But time ran out. Hundreds of thousands of combinations never got bought.

[shocked] Still... the winning ticket was in their pile. Twenty-seven million dollars.

[serious] The FBI and CIA investigated... and found nothing illegal.

[sly] So lotteries changed the rules... so nobody could ever do it again.

[playful] Would you have tried it? Comment yes or no... because this man won the lottery fourteen times... and it was completely legal.

## Fact check
- **He won the lottery 14 times.** ✔ (The Hustle; IFLScience; VnExpress; My Modern Met)
- **It was legal.** ✔ (My Modern Met: "perfectly legal"; IFLScience: investigated by the FBI and CIA, no wrongdoing found; The Hustle: investigated by 14 agencies, including the CIA, FBI and IRS, with no wrongdoing found)
- **The method:** the jackpot was worth more than buying every combination; he targeted jackpots worth at least 3 times the cost. ✔ (The Hustle; LotteryUSA)
- **Virginia, 1992: a $27 million jackpot.** ✔ (NPR; NZ Herald; Daily Herald; The Hustle: $27,036,142)
- **About $7 million to buy every combination.** ✔ (Daily Herald: $7.1 million; The Hustle: 7,059,052 combinations at $1 each)
- **They printed millions of tickets.** ✔ (NZ Herald and The Hustle: 30 computers and 12 laser printers in a Melbourne warehouse)
- **Couriers went store to store.** ✔ (The Hustle: 125 gas stations and supermarkets; NZ Herald and Daily Herald: three days to buy)
- **Time ran out, with hundreds of thousands of combinations left unbought.** ✔ (The Hustle: about 700,000 combinations unprocessed when a chain quit; NZ Herald: "his biggest jackpot still came down to luck")
- **They still won.** ✔ (NPR; The Hustle; NZ Herald)
- **The FBI and CIA investigated and found nothing illegal.** ✔ (The Hustle; IFLScience)
- **The rules were changed.** ✔ (The Hustle: Australia banned one person covering every combination, and all 44 US lottery states passed laws blocking it; VnExpress: "forcing rule changes")

**Kept out:**
- **His Romanian escape and the bribes.** The details come from one source.
- **His later bankruptcy and the prison sentence in Israel.** These are disputed, reportedly overturned, and off-story.
- **What investors received.** Sources give different numbers.
