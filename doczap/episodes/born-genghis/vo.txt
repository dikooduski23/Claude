You will be abandoned on the steppe with nothing. You will kill your own half-brother... over a fish. You will wear a wooden collar like a slave. And one day, the largest land empire the world has ever seen will carry your name. But tonight... you're just a baby. And legend says you were born holding blood.

It's around eleven sixty-two. Mongolia. Freezing wind, endless grass, felt tents. Your father is Yesügei, a small chief with a lot of enemies. Your mother is Hoelun... and he kidnapped her on her wedding day. That will matter later. When you're born, the old chronicle says, your tiny fist is clenched around a clot of blood the size of a knucklebone. Your father has just captured a Tatar warrior named Temüjin. So he names you after him. Temüjin. The elders whisper that you are marked. For greatness... or for bloodshed.

You're nine. Your father rides with you to find you a wife. A chief named Dai Setsen has a ten-year-old daughter... Börte. The fathers agree. You stay with her family, and your father rides home alone. On the way, he finds Tatars feasting. Steppe custom says you feed travelers. So he sits down. But someone recognizes him. The man who killed their people. They smile. They pour him a drink. He makes it home... dying. By the time you get back, your father is gone.

And then it gets worse. The clan won't follow a widow and her kids. So one morning... they pack up and leave. Everyone. They take the herds. You, your mother, your brothers and two half-brothers are left alone. With winter coming. Your mother refuses to let you die. She digs roots. You hunt marmots. You fish the river. Some days there is nothing to eat at all. Your little brothers cry from hunger, and you can't fix it. For the first time in your life... you are nobody. And nobody, on the steppe, is easy to kill.

You're about fourteen. And there's a problem. Your older half-brother, Behter. He's bigger. He keeps taking your food. One day, he takes a fish you caught. And something in you snaps. You and your brother Khasar take your bows. Khasar from the front. You from behind. Before he dies, Behter asks for one thing. Spare his little brother, Belgutei. You do. Your mother is furious with you. A family this small can't afford to lose anyone. But word travels on the steppe. And the people who abandoned you start to hear a name. Temüjin.

The Taichiud, the clan that abandoned you, hear Yesügei's son is getting dangerous. So they come for you. They catch you. They lock your neck and hands in a cangue, a heavy wooden collar, and pass you from tent to tent like an animal. Then one night... you slip away. You hide in the river, only your face above the water. A man named Sorkhan-Shira sees you in the moonlight... and says nothing. His family gives you a horse and a bow, and sends you home. Remember his name. Because the boy he just saved... is about to change the world.

You're sixteen now. You ride back for Börte, and her father keeps his word. Her dowry is a sable coat. And you know exactly what to do with it. You give it away. To Toghrul, khan of the Keraites, your father's old ally. A coat... for an army. Smart. Because soon after, at dawn, raiders hit your camp. The Merkits. Remember how your father stole your mother from them? They've come for revenge. You escape into the mountains. But Börte... is taken. So you call in the coat. Toghrul rides. Your childhood blood brother, Jamukha, rides. Thousands of warriors. You crush the Merkits, and in the chaos of the night, you run through the crowd shouting one name. Börte! Börte! And she hears you. Through the smoke and the screaming, she runs to your horse. Soon after, your first son is born. You name him Jochi.

But here's the twist. Jamukha, the friend who saved your wife... becomes your greatest enemy. He favors noble families. You do something crazy for the twelfth century. You promote by talent, not by blood. A herder's son can become a general. In one battle, an enemy archer shoots your war horse through the neck. After you win, you ask the prisoners... who shot my horse? One man steps forward. I did. Kill me, or spare me and I'll serve you. You spare him. You name him Jebe... the arrow. He becomes one of your greatest generals. That's why men leave Jamukha... and follow you. Years later, Jamukha's own men hand him over. You offer him his life. He refuses. The old chronicle says he asked for a noble death, without spilling his blood. And you granted it.

Spring, twelve-oh-six. The tribes gather. And the boy everyone abandoned is named ruler of them all. Genghis Khan. You break up the old clans into units of ten, a hundred, a thousand. You give your people a code of law. You allow every religion. You start horse relay stations, which your son will turn into a network across the empire. Then you ride. In twelve fifteen, you take the Jin capital, where Beijing stands today. In twelve eighteen, you send about four hundred fifty merchants west, to trade with the Khwarazm Empire. The governor of Otrar calls them spies... and kills them all. One camel driver escapes. You send envoys to the Shah for justice. He beheads one, and shaves the beards of the others. Within three years... his empire collapses. Cities are wiped out. Your armies cross mountains no one thought they could cross. This is the part of your story the world never forgets. And the man who started it... has only a few years left.

Twelve twenty-seven. You're in your sixties, at war again, against the Western Xia. And in August... you die. How? Nobody knows for sure. Every story about it was written later. Even your grave is a mystery. Nearly eight hundred years later, no one has found it. In two thousand three, a study claimed one in two hundred men alive today might descend from you. Newer research says that bloodline... might not be yours at all. But the empire you started became the largest connected land empire in history.

A baby holding blood. A boy left with nothing. A prisoner in a wooden collar. Who ended up ruling more land than anyone before him. So... were you a builder of nations, or a destroyer of them? Tell me in the comments. And tell me who should be born next.
