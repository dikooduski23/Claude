But how does an army of herders beat empires? It starts in childhood. Mongol kids learn to ride at around three, and soon they're shooting arrows from a moving horse. Your soldiers don't need roads or supply wagons. Each rider brings three or four spare horses. When one gets tired, he jumps to the next... and keeps going for days. Their bow is short, made of wood, horn and sinew glued together, and it shoots farther than most bows of its time. And their favorite trick? Pretend to lose. Your riders turn and run. The enemy cheers... and chases. Straight into a trap, where the rest of your army is waiting. First, you test them on the Western Xia kingdom. After a string of sieges, by twelve ten, their king gives in. Then, in twelve eleven, you invade the Jin dynasty in northern China. Their army waits for you in a mountain pass called Badger's Mouth. Their commander hesitates... and you crush them. Thousands die. Then, in twelve fifteen, you take the Jin capital, where Beijing stands today.
