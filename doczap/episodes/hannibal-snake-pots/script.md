**Cutaway:** a sealed clay jar sliced open, with venomous snakes coiled inside and a wax-sealed lid. Handwritten label: "snakes".
**Length:** about 104 words, roughly 38–42 s with Patrick.

**What I deliberately left out:**
- **The year.** It's usually given as about 184 BC, but some scholars say 186 BC. The script says no year.
- **"The night before."** No source says when the snakes were caught, so the script says "before the battle".
- **A number of snakes.** No source gives one, so the script says "as many as they could find".
- **Hannibal finding Eumenes's own ship.** Nepos says he sent a herald to spot the king's ship. It's true, but I cut it for time.

**Caveat:** all three accounts are ancient Roman writers. Modern historians accept that the battle happened but treat the snake detail as reported, not proven. That's normal for ancient history, and the script never claims anything beyond what they wrote.

## Sources

2. Justin, *Epitome of the Philippic History of Pompeius Trogus*, book 32, chapter 4.
3. Sextus Julius Frontinus, *Stratagems*, book 4, chapter 7, sections 10–11.
